How to use PanRes

PanRes links curated resistance genes to their translated proteins, predicted structures, sequence clusters, structure clusters, 3D-based alignments, and HMM profiles. The database can be searched directly or browsed through the main categories on the home page.

What the PanRes terms mean

  • PanGene — a curated resistance gene entry. This is the best starting point for resistance annotations such as phenotype, resistance class, mechanism, and source database.
  • PanProtein — the protein translation of a PanGene. Protein pages connect gene entries to protein clusters and predicted structures.
  • PanGeneCluster 🏷pan_cl#### — a group of closely related genes clustered at 90% sequence identity and 90% coverage.
  • PanProteinCluster 🏷PANCL#### — a group of related proteins clustered at 50% sequence identity and 90% coverage.
  • PanStructure — an AlphaFold prediction for a representative protein sequence.
  • PanStructureCluster 🏷PANCL####_struct — a group of proteins with similar predicted structures. These clusters are used to provide 3D-based multiple sequence alignments and HMM profiles.

Search and browse

Use the search box to find a specific gene, cluster, resistance class, phenotype, mechanism, or source database. Use the category buttons to browse broader groups.

The PanRes Genes table is the most useful overview. It can be filtered by annotation, cluster membership, and E. coli core homolog status.

E. coli core homologs

Some functional metagenomics entries may resemble common E. coli genes rather than specific resistance determinants. These are marked as E. coli core homologs when the protein matches the soft-core E. coli genome at 60% identity and 60% coverage.

The gene table excludes these by default, but you can include them or show only these entries using the E. coli core homolog filter.

Acquired resistance genes

PanGenes that are represented in ResFinder are marked with an Acquired label on their gene page.

This is a source-based PanRes label: it indicates that the gene is present in ResFinder and is intended to make established acquired resistance determinants easy to recognize while browsing.

Sequences and structures

Gene and protein pages link the biological entry to its sequence, cluster membership, and predicted structure when available.

Structure pages include an interactive 3D viewer, so you can rotate the model, change the representation, and download the predicted PDB file.

Structure clusters, MSAs, and HMMs

Structure cluster pages are useful when you want to compare proteins that may be distant at the sequence level but similar in predicted 3D structure.

Use View MSA to inspect the 3D-based multiple sequence alignment, or Download HMM to download the profile for the current cluster.

Downloads

From the PanRes Genes table, you can download gene FASTA, protein FASTA, or metadata for the rows currently shown by your filters.

The full PanRes dataset and related resources can be accessed on Zenodo.
